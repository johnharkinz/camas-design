For this round, do **not** create four new directions and do **not** combine the previous concepts into one design.

Develop two existing directions independently:

## A2 — Index, with more character

Take Direction A — Index — as the starting point.

Keep the things that worked well:

- restrained, calm and precise overall feel
- strong hierarchy
- catalogue/index idea
- numbering as a way of organising the growing tool family
- warm paper/ink palette
- limited accent colour
- excellent behaviour at browser-extension size
- mono used for data and labels rather than as generic “developer” styling
- square corners, rules and compact information structure

The main problem to solve is that it risks becoming tasteful but anonymous.

Push this direction so it feels more specifically like **Camas**, without making it louder, more decorative or more SaaS-like.

Explore where additional character could come from through typography, composition, identity, graphic language, rhythm or other system-level choices.

Do not solve this simply by adding more colour, illustration or decoration.

Show:

- refined Camas identity
- camas.dev homepage
- representative Shareable result
- realistic Noggin-style 360 × 540 extension popup
- updated typography, colour, controls and design-language treatment

Explain what changed from A and why.

## D2 — Instrument, with less literal skeuomorphism

Take Direction D — Instrument — as the starting point.

Keep the things that worked well:

- the idea that Camas tools are instruments for inspecting and changing the web
- very clear state communication
- strong extension UI
- tactile interaction language
- the principle that raised surfaces are pressable and inset surfaces are inputs/readouts
- restrained use of indicator lights
- sense of precision and usefulness
- warm neutral palette

The main problem to solve is that the metaphor becomes too literal when applied to the whole Camas identity and marketing site.

Reduce the “piece of laboratory/bench equipment” styling without losing its functional interaction language.

The website should feel like the home of well-made tools, not like the website itself is pretending to be a physical instrument.

Keep tactility where it improves understanding, especially in controls, states and compact product UI.

Show:

- refined Camas identity
- camas.dev homepage
- representative Shareable result
- realistic Noggin-style 360 × 540 extension popup
- updated typography, colour, controls and design-language treatment

Explain what changed from D and why.

## Product-language principle to carry into both

There is one important idea from Direction B — Field Guide — that should be tested in both A2 and D2:

**Technical findings should be explained in clear, plain English before exposing implementation detail.**

For example, prefer:

“The X card image is missing”

followed by the relevant technical detail,

rather than making `twitter:image` the primary thing the user has to interpret.

Carry this principle into Shareable particularly.

Do **not** bring the botanical, herbarium or “Plate I / Plate II” visual theme into A2 or D2.

## Important constraints

Keep A2 and D2 genuinely separate.

Do not average them together.

Do not try to produce a final Camas design yet.

The purpose of this round is to see whether:

- A can gain enough distinctive Camas character while retaining its restraint
- D can lose enough literal equipment styling while retaining its excellent product interaction language

Prioritise quality and coherence over producing lots of variants.

Use the same representative content in both directions so that the design language can be compared fairly.
