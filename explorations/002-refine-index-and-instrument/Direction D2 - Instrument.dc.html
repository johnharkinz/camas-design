ROUND 2 — DEVELOPING D
Instrument, in the hand
The equipment metaphor now lives only where you touch it. The brand and site are plain, light and editorial; the tools keep the raised keys, inset readouts and status lamps that make their state unmistakable.
WHAT CHANGED FROM D, AND WHY
1Depth is a rule, not a style. Only things you can press are raised and only things that show or take values are inset. Pages, sections and cards are flat paper — so depth always means something.
2The site shows the tools; it isn’t one. No rack panels or dark hero readout. The homepage presents real, working controls from each product as the product imagery.
3The lamp became a signal. The orange dot is no longer a power light on a panel. It means one thing system-wide: “this is live”. In the wordmark, Camas is the thing that’s on.
4Gone: model numbers, tracked engraved caps, enamel grey backgrounds, dark readout screens. Labels are sentence case; the background is paper, not metal.
5Findings read before they measure. Plain-English headline first; the technical reading sits in an inset strip beneath, exactly like a value display.
Still a risk: with the metaphor removed from marketing, the site carries less identity on its own; character now depends on the product UI being on show.
